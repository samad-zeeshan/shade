02 The problem

The ban ends
at 15:00.
The heat does not.

Outdoor workers in the Gulf work through months of summer heat. The law stops their work from 12:30 to 15:00. Outside those hours crews can still be over the heat limit, and a planner has no way to see where shade falls.

03 How it works

Three steps,
every 15 minutes.

  1. 01

    Find the shade

    Real building outlines and the path of the sun give the shadow at each work spot every 15 minutes.

  2. 02

    Set a heat limit

    A heat model from published studies turns the weather and the shade into a heat limit for each spot. The model uses a heat index called WBGT. It adds up the air heat, the sun, the wind and the humidity.

  3. 03

    Place the crews

    A scheduler puts each crew where and when work is allowed.

05 Who it is for

For the site planner
and the safety officer.

  1. A

    See where work is allowed

    The map shows where a crew may work on the plot at any time of day.

  2. B

    Plan crews around the shade

    The plan moves each crew into shadow as the shadow moves across the site.

  3. C

    Hand out each crew's plan

    Each crew's plan downloads as a spreadsheet with times and places.
